Notice

Player's Tome

  • Move: Click the golden highlighted tiles, or press arrow keys / WASD.
  • Attack: Click an adjacent monster to strike it in melee (once per round).
  • Shoot: With a bow or sling loaded, click a monster inside the red circle.
  • Doors: Closed doors block movement. Click a door next to you, or press a direction into it, to open it.
  • Search traps: Thieves can click Find Traps, then click an adjacent tile.
  • Cast spells: Click a spell button, then click a target. Healing and buff spells target yourself.
  • Rest: Click the Rest button in a safe area to recover HP. It consumes your action.
  • End Turn: Click End Turn when you are done acting; enemies then take their turn.
  • Items: Potions and scrolls can be used from the action bar or from your character sheet.
  • Ability Scores: Strength, Intelligence, Wisdom, Dexterity, Constitution, Charisma. High scores grant modifiers to hit points, attacks, AC, and more.
  • AC (Armour Class): In OSRIC, lower AC is better. A base AC 10 person in plate mail might be AC 3. The UI also shows the modern ascending value in parentheses.
  • THAC0: "To Hit Armour Class 0". To hit an enemy, roll d20 and meet or exceed THAC0 minus the target's descending AC. The server resolves this for you.
  • HP: Hit points. Reaching 0 renders you unconscious; negative HP risks death. Rest, potions, or healing spells restore HP.
  • Saves: Saving throws resist traps, poison, breath weapons, and spells. Roll d20 and meet or exceed your save number.
  • Morale: Monsters may flee when wounded or when half their group falls. Bosses usually stand firm.
  • Surprise: Each encounter rolls surprise. A surprised side skips its first round.
  • Initiative: Each round both sides roll d6, plus DEX and weapon speed. The higher roll acts first.
  • XP & Leveling: Defeat monsters and loot gold to earn XP. Reach the listed XP threshold to gain a level, more HP, and better saves.
  • Fighter — High HP, all armour and weapons. Simple and durable. Recommended for beginners.
  • Cleric — Wears armour, heals allies, and turns undead. No edged weapons.
  • Magic-User — Powerful spells but fragile; no armour and poor weapons.
  • Thief — Sneaks, finds traps, and backstabs. Light armour only.
  • Druid — Nature magic, healing, and useful summoning. Cannot use metal armour.
  • Paladin — Holy warrior with healing and undead turning. High ability requirements.
  • Ranger — Skilled warrior with nature magic and bonuses against certain foes.
  • Assassin — Sneaks and backstabs like a thief but fights better. Evil alignments only.
  • Monk — Unarmed combatant with high saves and mobility. No armour.
  • Bard — Jack of all trades: some thief skills, some magic, and inspiration.
  • Round structure: Each round both sides roll initiative. The winning side acts first, then the other, then the next round begins.
  • Movement: Your movement in tiles is shown on the turn badge. Moving consumes movement; diagonal movement is not used.
  • Attacks: Each character can make one attack action per round, whether melee, ranged, a spell, or a class ability.
  • Spell casting: Casting a spell counts as your attack. If you are hit while casting, the spell may be lost.
  • Turning undead: Clerics and paladins can attempt to drive away visible undead instead of attacking.
  • Thief skills: Sneaking and trap-finding count as your action for the round.
  • Resting: In a safe area, resting recovers HP but ends your turn. Wandering monsters may interrupt rest.
  • Death and dying: At 0 HP you fall unconscious and roll a death save each turn. Stabilise at 0 HP, then rest or heal.
